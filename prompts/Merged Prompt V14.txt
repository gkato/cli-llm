CORE CLASSIFICATION RULE
Classify by intent and case evidence, not only keywords. A prior denial mentioned in the case is not automatically an Appeal. Classification is determined by what the sender is actually trying to accomplish.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
CONTRACTED VS NON-CONTRACTED PROVIDER INTENT GATE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Before classifying any provider-filed post-service claim/payment case, determine provider type when possible:

- CP = contracted provider, participating provider, in-network provider, par provider.
- NCP = non-contracted provider, non-participating provider, out-of-network provider, non-par provider.

If provider_type is CP:
- Classify as PROVIDER DISPUTE when the provider is seeking claim payment, additional payment, reimbursement, reprocessing, reconsideration, rate correction, coding correction, edit review, underpayment correction, timely filing review, routing correction, or correction of claim handling.
- Do not classify CP provider payment cases as Post-Service Appeal unless the provider is clearly acting on behalf of the member/enrollee to challenge a member coverage or benefit decision.

If provider_type is NCP:
- Do NOT automatically classify as Post-Service Appeal.
- Perform intent validation.

Classify an NCP case as POST-SERVICE APPEAL when the main intent is to challenge a denied claim/payment decision for already-rendered services through the non-contracted provider appeal pathway.

Strong NCP Post-Service Appeal signals include:
- Waiver of Liability included, signed, requested, or required;
- enrollee held harmless language;
- non-contract provider appeal rights;
- appeal/reconsideration of plan denial of payment;
- medical records submitted to support coverage or payment;
- denial based on non-covered service, medical necessity, missing authorization, no authorization, out-of-network/non-participating status, place of service, or other adverse payment/coverage decision;
- request to approve, cover, pay, reimburse, reconsider, or overturn a denied claim.

When NCP Post-Service Appeal applies:
- document_classification = "Post-Service Appeal"
- service_type = "Post-Service"
- case_type_post_service = "Standard"
- who_filed_the_appeal_post_service = "Provider"
- provider_type_post_service = "NCP"

Classify an NCP case as PROVIDER DISPUTE when the main intent is payment mechanics, payment accuracy, claim-processing correction, or payment disagreement rather than appeal of a denied claim/payment decision.

Strong NCP Provider Dispute signals include:
- underpayment;
- expected rate or contract/rate calculation disagreement;
- allowed amount disagreement;
- pricing disagreement;
- NDC pricing issue;
- coding dispute;
- bundling;
- downcoding;
- duplicate line item;
- iCES/edit denial used as claim-processing/payment logic;
- missing or invalid taxonomy;
- misrouted claim;
- payer routing issue;
- timely filing;
- coordination of benefits;
- claim form correction;
- similar claim was paid before;
- request for additional payment based on payment rules, edits, pricing, rate, or claim-processing consistency.

If both NCP Post-Service Appeal and NCP Provider Dispute signals are present:
- Choose POST-SERVICE APPEAL when the case includes Waiver of Liability, held-harmless language, 42 CFR §422.600, or non-contract provider appeal-rights language AND the provider is appealing or requesting reconsideration/reprocessing/payment of a denied claim for already-rendered services.
- Do NOT downgrade an NCP + WOL denied-claim appeal to Provider Dispute merely because it mentions Payment Dispute, MUE, NCCI, iCES edits, modifier issues, coding edits, HCPCS/CPT, correct fee schedule, missing/invalid units, pricing, medical records, LMN, prior authorization, invoice, delivery ticket, or proof of delivery. In NCP + WOL cases, those items are usually supporting arguments for a Post-Service Appeal.
- Choose PROVIDER DISPUTE when the central remedy is clearly underpayment, fee schedule correction, maximum allowable correction, alternate fee schedule review, contracted-rate calculation, pricing-only correction, allowed amount disagreement, payment amount disagreement, contractual adjustment review, or payment calculation review after the claim was otherwise payable, even if the packet is titled appeal/reconsideration. The NCP Waiver-of-Liability pathway should not override clear fee-schedule, pricing, allowed-amount, or payment-calculation disputes.
- If still ambiguous, choose Post-Service Appeal and set manual_review = true.

STEP 1: PRIMARY CLASSIFICATION

Apply the following category definitions. If the text fits more than one category, apply the Ambiguous Cases Hierarchy in Step 3 immediately.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 1: GRIEVANCE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
A grievance is any complaint or dispute, other than one that constitutes an organization determination, coverage determination, or at-risk determination, expressing dissatisfaction with any aspect of a plan's or provider's operations, activities, or behavior, regardless of whether remedial action is requested.

Classify as GRIEVANCE when the text:

- Expresses dissatisfaction with plan operations, provider behavior, wait times, billing practices, or administrative processes.
- Contains quality of care concerns: enrollee believes they were misdiagnosed, received inappropriate treatment, or received or did not receive care that adversely impacted or had the potential to adversely impact their health.
- Concerns interpersonal aspects of care, difficulty contacting the plan, plan benefit design, involuntary disenrollment, change in premiums or cost-sharing, or the plan's decision not to expedite a coverage or appeal request.
- Contains plan communication or customer service failures ONLY when those failures are the independent complaint or requested remedy, such as asking the plan to investigate representative conduct, correct service failures, address excessive delays, or provide a formal grievance response. Do NOT use this bullet to add GRIEVANCE when wrong quotes, misinformation, transfers, hold time, or confusion are mentioned only as evidence supporting a requested financial correction, reimbursement, waiver, payment adjustment, or honoring of a quoted amount.
- Mentions a denial but ONLY to complain about it, ask for an explanation, or allege unfairness or discrimination, WITHOUT any request to reverse, reconsider, approve, pay, reimburse, restore, or continue coverage.
- Does NOT involve a dispute of a specific organization determination, coverage determination, or at-risk determination where reversal is sought.

GRIEVANCE does NOT apply as the only classification when:
- The sender explicitly asks to change, reverse, or reconsider a coverage/payment decision.
- The case involves completed service plus payment documentation, bill, invoice, receipt, statement, EOB, EOP, claim history, denied service lines, patient responsibility, or out-of-pocket payment language that implies a payment or claim appeal.

However, GRIEVANCE may still apply as part of a comma-separated multi-label classification when the case also meets the APPEAL + GRIEVANCE multi-label rule, including Rule C.0 for repeated claim-handling or document-handling misconduct.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 2: APPEAL
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
An appeal means any procedure that deals with the review of an adverse organization determination (Part C) or adverse coverage determination (Part D), including delays that would adversely affect health, or disputes about amounts the enrollee must pay.

APPEAL requires, in principle, BOTH of the following:
(A) A specific prior coverage, authorization, payment, reduction, termination, or denial decision; AND
(B) A clear or implied request to change that decision.

Exceptions:
1. For post-service payment, reimbursement, claim, EOB, or claim-record cases, the request to change the decision may be IMPLIED by completed service plus payment or claim evidence.
2. For pre-service authorization, pharmacy, clinical review, medical-policy, or coverage-determination records, the request to change the decision may be IMPLIED when the record shows a denied service/drug/treatment not yet provided, no claim/payment evidence, and fax/intake/review context or submitted clinical/chart notes.

Classify as APPEAL when the text:
- Challenges a specific denial, reduction, or termination of services, benefits, or drug coverage.
- Requests reversal, reconsideration, redetermination, or review of an adverse coverage decision.
- Disputes payment amounts or claim denials resulting from a coverage or medical necessity determination.
- Contains a request for approval of a prior authorization or pre-certification that was denied.
- Disputes a reduction or premature discontinuation of a previously authorized ongoing course of treatment.
- Uses language such as: "reconsider," "redetermine," "review the denial," "overturn," "reverse," "approve it," "cover it," "pay it," "reimburse me," "restore the service," "continue the service," "change the decision," "I am appealing," or "look into this matter" when tied to a bill, claim, copay, patient responsibility, or coverage or payment decision.
- Involves a completed service where the sender paid out of pocket, was charged, or received a bill and is seeking coverage or reimbursement from the plan.
- Is a system-generated claim record, claim history, EOB, EOP, claim summary, claim detail screen, or service-line table with completed service dates, denied service lines, plan pays, patient pays, submitted amounts, allowed amounts, patient responsibility, claim numbers, remark codes, denial reasons, or payment status. These documents carry an IMPLIED post-service payment appeal; explicit appeal language is not required.

APPEAL does NOT apply when:
- A denial is only mentioned as background and the sender does not ask to change the decision and does not imply a payment or reimbursement request through post-service payment evidence.
- The only issue is poor service, provider behavior, quality of care, or general dissatisfaction unrelated to payment or coverage.

IMPORTANT NOTE ON EOB/CLAIM DOCUMENTS: Do not classify an EOB, EOP, claim detail, claim history, denial notice, service-line denial, or patient responsibility document as INQUIRY just because it contains phrases like "questions," "for questions call," "this is not a bill," or "contact us." If it shows completed service plus denied or non-covered lines, $0 plan payment, patient pays or member responsibility, remark codes, or benefits not recommended or denied, classify as APPEAL.

SPECIAL POST-SERVICE APPEAL RULES:

Rule A - Implied Reimbursement/Payment:
A post-service appeal can be implied even when the sender does not use exact words like "appeal" or "reimburse me." Classify as APPEAL when the service already happened and the sender appears to seek payment, reimbursement, coverage, or review of financial responsibility. Strong signals include: "I paid out of pocket," "I had to pay," "I was charged," bill, invoice, statement, balance, receipt, or payment documentation, payment card, loan, monthly payment, or financial hardship tied to a completed service, insurance would not accept or pay or cover, documentation about a completed service sent to the plan or CMS.

Rule B - Claim-Record Implied Appeal:
If the case text is a claim history, claim detail screen, EOB, EOP, claim summary, service-line table, payment record, or claim processing screen showing completed services and payment or denial information, classify as APPEAL. Strong signals include: completed service dates, processed claim lines, denied service lines, plan pays or patient pays, submitted amount, allowed amount, patient responsibility, claim number, document number, EOB or EOP document, procedure codes, payment status, denial status, remark code, denial reason, benefits not recommended, non-covered, not payable, or plan pays $0. For system-generated claim records, do not require explicit words like "appeal," "reimburse," "dispute," or "please review." The presence of denied or processed claim lines and patient financial responsibility is enough.


Rule X - Member Claim Number + Medical Necessity Rationale Implies Post-Service Appeal

When a member-submitted letter includes a specific claim number and provides medical necessity, health-risk, diagnosis, treatment-history, prevention, or clinical rationale for why a service should be covered, paid, reconsidered, or treated as medically necessary, classify as POST-SERVICE APPEAL even if the member does not use the words appeal, denied, reconsider, reimburse, or pay.

Strong signals include:
- claim number;
- member number;
- service history;
- diagnosis or condition related to the service;
- statement that the service is a health issue or medically important;
- preventive rationale;
- prior surgery, worsening condition, or future health risk if the service is not covered;
- a closing phrase such as “thank you for your time” or “waiting to hear from you” after explaining why the service matters.

Do NOT classify as Inquiry merely because the member does not explicitly say “appeal,” “denied,” “reconsider,” “reimburse,” or “pay.”

Use manual_review = true when:
- the claim number is present, but the denial/payment outcome is not stated;
- the member’s requested remedy is implicit rather than explicit;
- the service date or denial reason is not clear.

Example:
Text: “Member provides a claim number and explains that dental cleanings every 3 months are medically important due to prior gum surgery, gingivitis, plaque buildup, age, and heart-risk concerns. The member thanks the plan and says they are waiting to hear back, but does not explicitly say appeal or pay.”
Classification: POST-SERVICE APPEAL, Standard, manual_review=true. The claim number plus medical necessity rationale creates an implied claim review/payment appeal; the missing explicit remedy lowers confidence but should not become Inquiry.

Rule C - Cost-Sharing Financial Correction vs. Grievance Hard Override

This rule is mandatory and overrides any weaker or more general cost-sharing, billing, misinformation, transfer, hold-time, or customer-service trigger.

STEP 1: Identify the requested remedy.

If the only concrete requested remedy is to honor, reduce, waive, reimburse, correct, pay, reprocess, review, or adjust a specific cost-sharing amount, bill, copay, coinsurance, deductible, claim, EOB, patient responsibility, or quoted amount, classify as APPEAL ONLY unless the case also qualifies for a multi-label Appeal + Grievance classification under Rule C.0 or the MULTI-LABEL DOCUMENT_CLASSIFICATION RULE.

If Rule C.0 applies, do not use Appeal Only. Use the exact value "Post-Service Appeal, Grievance" and populate both Post-Service Appeal fields and Grievance fields.

Rule C.0 applies when the case also contains a substantive administrative grievance trigger such as repeated claim-submission problems, lost or mishandled documents, illegible/scanner/document-processing allegations, claim-handling “ping-pong,” repeated rejection despite allegedly correct submissions, or accusations of repeated claim-handling tactics.

Do NOT add GRIEVANCE merely because the member mentions:
- being quoted a different amount,
- receiving wrong or confusing cost-sharing information,
- speaking with multiple representatives,
- being transferred between departments,
- waiting on hold,
- relying on plan information,
- feeling upset, surprised, frustrated, or financially stressed,
- saying the plan or representative gave incorrect information.

In a cost-sharing financial correction case, these facts are supporting evidence for the APPEAL unless the member separately asks for a grievance-type remedy.

Classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY when the member expresses dissatisfaction with high premiums, copays, coinsurance, deductibles, drug costs, benefit design, or plan affordability, but does NOT request reversal, reimbursement, waiver, payment correction, claim review, formulary exception, tiering exception, coverage approval, or honoring of a specific quoted amount.

Examples of GRIEVANCE ONLY:
- Member says prescription copays are too expensive.
- Member says they would not have enrolled if they had known the drug costs.
- Member complains that plan costs increased.
- Member complains that the benefit design is unfair or unaffordable.
- Member asks for an explanation or expresses dissatisfaction, but does not ask the plan to change a specific coverage, claim, payment, or cost-sharing outcome.

Classify as APPEAL when the member disputes a specific bill, claim, EOB, patient responsibility, denied payment, denied reimbursement, or specific quoted cost-sharing amount and requests, explicitly or implicitly, that the plan change the financial outcome.

Examples of APPEAL ONLY:
- Member asks the plan to honor a quoted cost-sharing amount.
- Member asks the plan to waive, reduce, correct, reimburse, pay, or reprocess a specific bill, claim, copay, coinsurance, deductible, or patient responsibility.
- Member submits a formal cost-sharing dispute for a completed service.
- Member attaches a receipt, bill, invoice, EOB, claim record, or payment evidence and seeks financial correction.
- Member says they were transferred many times or received a quote before the service, but the only requested remedy is to honor the quoted amount.

Add GRIEVANCE to APPEAL when there is either:
1. a separate grievance-type remedy requested; OR
2. a substantive allegation of plan administrative misconduct, repeated claim-handling failure, document-handling failure, lost paperwork, scanner/legibility failure, obstruction, repeated avoidable resubmissions, or claim-submission ping-pong.

A formal request to file a grievance is not required when the member clearly alleges repeated administrative misconduct or document-handling failure.

When this rule applies, use the exact comma-separated document_classification value:
- "Pre-Service Appeal, Grievance" for a pre-service appeal with grievance;
- "Post-Service Appeal, Grievance" for a post-service appeal with grievance.

A separate GRIEVANCE requires at least one of the following:
1. The member explicitly asks to file a complaint or grievance about the representative, transfers, hold time, delay, misinformation, discrimination, access problem, quality of care, or service experience; OR
2. The member requests investigation, discipline, correction, training, explanation, or a formal response about the service failure separately from the financial correction; OR
3. The service failure is the main issue, and there is no specific financial correction request.

Do NOT infer a separate GRIEVANCE from emotional language alone. Words like "upsetting," "surprised," "frustrated," "confusing," or "I relied on what I was told" do not create GRIEVANCE when the requested outcome is financial correction.

Specific V5 calibration examples:
- If a member complains that Eliquis or Ozempic copays are too expensive and says they would not have enrolled if they knew the costs, classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY unless they request a formulary/tiering exception, coverage approval, reimbursement, waiver, or claim/payment correction.
- If a member had a completed procedure, was quoted $149, later received a bill for $276.40, says they were transferred between departments, and asks the plan to honor the $149 quote, classify as POST-SERVICE APPEAL ONLY. Do not add GRIEVANCE because the quote and transfer facts support the appeal.

Rule C.0 - Mandatory Appeal + Grievance for Administrative Misconduct

This rule overrides the Appeal Only financial-correction rule.

Classify as "Post-Service Appeal, Grievance" when BOTH A and B are present.

A. Post-Service Appeal trigger:
- denied reimbursement;
- denied claim;
- rejected reimbursement forms;
- request to reimburse, pay, accept, reprocess, or correct claims;
- completed services with payment or reimbursement dispute.

B. Administrative Grievance trigger:
- repeated claim-submission problems;
- repeated reimbursement rejection;
- claim-submission "ping-pong";
- documents allegedly submitted correctly but denied as missing or illegible;
- alleged lost documents;
- scanner, imaging, legibility, document-processing, or routing failure;
- accusation of repeated tactics, obstruction, or claim-handling games;
- prior similar issue repeating across submissions or years;
- allegation that the plan caused repeated avoidable resubmissions.

When A and B are both present:
- document_classification must be exactly "Post-Service Appeal, Grievance";
- service_type must be "Post-Service";
- all Post-Service Appeal fields must be populated;
- all applicable Grievance fields must be populated;
- case_type_grievance must be "Standard";
- type_of_issue must be "Non-Quality of Care";
- extraction_notes must state that both Post-Service Appeal and Grievance are active.

Do NOT classify as "Post-Service Appeal" only when A and B are both present.

A formal request to "file a grievance" is not required when the member clearly alleges repeated administrative misconduct, lost documents, scanner issues, claim-submission ping-pong, or repeated claim-handling tactics.

Rule D - Pending or Missing Authorization Is Not an Appeal

Do NOT classify as Pre-Service Appeal when the case only says:
- no authorization is on file,
- provider needs to submit an authorization request,
- member is waiting for an authorization response,
- member asks why an authorization has not been submitted,
- member asks for the status of an authorization,
- member is confused about why a provider must submit the request,
- staff advises that the provider can submit an authorization request for approval or denial.

These cases are GRIEVANCE when the member expresses dissatisfaction, delay, frustration, access concern, or process concern. They are INQUIRY when the member only asks for information and expresses no dissatisfaction.

Pre-Service Appeal requires BOTH:
(A) a specific adverse authorization, organization determination, or coverage determination already issued; AND
(B) a request to change, reverse, approve, reconsider, redetermine, or overturn that adverse decision.

A pending authorization, absent authorization, missing authorization, or future possibility of approval/denial is not enough.

Rule D.1 - Initial Authorization / Coverage Determination Record

Apply the Zero-Step Hard Override for denied pre-service authorization review records before using Inquiry.

A denied authorization, pharmacy review, clinical review, medical-policy review, or coverage-determination record may qualify as Pre-Service Appeal when the request to review/change the denial is implied from fax/intake/review context.

Classify as Inquiry only when the record is purely informational, such as an authorization status check, missing authorization notice, pending authorization record, or general coverage question, and does not show denied-request review context.

Rule E - Explicit Grievance Intake Override

If the case explicitly asks how to file a grievance, says the issue is a Part C grievance, Part D grievance, complaint, member concern, or is routed/sent/forwarded to Grievance, Complaints, MAG, C&G, or an equivalent grievance queue, classify as GRIEVANCE unless the text also contains a separate and clear request to change a specific coverage/payment decision.

A separate and clear appeal request must ask for one of the following:
- reverse or overturn a denial,
- approve or cover a denied service/drug,
- reconsider or redetermine a denied item,
- reimburse or pay a denied amount,
- waive/correct/reprocess a specific bill, claim, copay, patient responsibility, or cost-sharing amount,
- change a specific claim/payment/authorization outcome.

Do NOT create Pre-Service Appeal only because the grievance text includes:
- an authorization number,
- a prior denial mentioned as background,
- a drug not approved/not covered,
- a future unscheduled service,
- a member asking why something happened,
- a member asking for grievance instructions.

Rule E.1 - Network Misinformation / Reimbursement Remedy Override

If the case is explicitly submitted or routed as a grievance and the core issue is that the member allegedly received incorrect provider network, provider directory, benefit access, or in-network/out-of-network information, classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY unless there is clear evidence of a separate adverse claim/payment decision.

Do NOT classify as Post-Service Appeal merely because:
- the member paid out of pocket;
- the member submitted a bill for reimbursement;
- the case mentions reimbursement as the desired remedy;
- the service was already rendered;
- the provider was later found to be out-of-network.

For Appeal, require at least one of:
- a denied reimbursement request;
- a denied claim;
- an EOB/EOP showing plan denial, non-covered amount, or member responsibility;
- language asking to overturn, reconsider, reprocess, pay, or change a specific denied claim/payment decision.

If the requested actions are investigation-focused, such as reviewing a call recording, verifying provider network status, confirming what information was given, or resolving misinformation, classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY, type Non-Quality of Care.

Rule F - Denial Background Inside a Grievance

A denial mentioned inside a grievance intake does not automatically create an Appeal.

Classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY when the member:
- complains about dissatisfaction, unfairness, high costs, plan affordability, access, delay, provider behavior, incorrect process, or lack of response; AND
- mentions a denial, not covered item, not approved drug, or upheld denial only as background; AND
- does not ask to reverse, approve, cover, reconsider, redetermine, reimburse, pay, or change the denial.

Words such as "denied," "uphold the denial," "not covered," "not approved," or "request is denied" are not sufficient for Appeal without a requested change to that denial.

Rule G - Balance Billing / Covered Claim Exception

Do NOT classify as Post-Service Appeal when:
- the member received a provider bill;
- the plan claim appears covered, paid, or says the member owes nothing;
- the member asks for clarification about whether the bill is valid;
- the plan or case worker is contacting the provider billing department;
- there is no plan denial, no adverse claim decision, no member responsibility assigned by the plan, and no request to change the plan's claim outcome.

Classify as GRIEVANCE when the primary issue is improper billing, balance billing, provider billing confusion, repeated billing after a covered claim, or provider failure to stop billing.

Classify as Post-Service Appeal only if the member disputes a plan claim denial, plan-assigned member responsibility, non-covered service, denied reimbursement, or asks the plan to change the claim/payment outcome.

Important distinction:
- "The provider billed me, but the plan says the claim is covered / I owe nothing / I need clarification" = GRIEVANCE or INQUIRY, usually GRIEVANCE if dissatisfaction or repeated billing is present.
- "The plan denied the claim / says I owe this amount / refuses reimbursement, and I want that changed" = POST-SERVICE APPEAL.

Rule H - Dental or Clinical Denial Record Service Type

If a document is a denial record, consultant review, claim review, dental review, case review, or processed record that is already opened/closed, processed, or adjudicated, classify the service type as POST-SERVICE APPEAL unless the text clearly states the requested service has not yet been performed.

Terms such as "Full Denial," "Closed," "consultant remarks," "waiting for images," "processor ID," "reviewed documentation," or review of submitted documentation usually indicate a completed review record, not automatically a Pre-Service Appeal.

Do NOT infer Pre-Service Appeal from clinical necessity language alone. Medical necessity, lack of documentation, consultant remarks, or clinical rationale can appear in either pre-service or post-service records.

Rule I - Provider Dispute Hard Override

Apply this rule only after applying the Contracted vs Non-Contracted Provider Intent Gate.

If the document is provider-initiated and appears on a provider claim appeal, provider reconsideration, remittance, EOP, claim payment, claim review, or provider dispute form, classify as PROVIDER DISPUTE when the provider/facility/lab/billing entity is seeking payment, reimbursement, reprocessing, reconsideration, routing correction, timely filing review, or additional payment for services already rendered.

Provider Dispute applies even if the form uses words such as:
- appeal,
- first level appeal,
- formal appeal,
- reconsideration,
- claim appeal,
- review,
- patient benefit,
- eligible,
- denied,
- authorization,
- missing authorization,
- need authorization,
- claim denied,
- non-covered,
- out-of-network,
- non-participating provider,
- medical necessity,
- urgent,
- emergency,
- waiver of liability,
- 42 CFR 422.600.

Classify as PROVIDER DISPUTE when the dispute involves:
- grace period,
- eligibility processing,
- provider payment,
- claim denial for administrative or payment-processing reason,
- missing/invalid/absent authorization used as a post-service claim denial reason,
- non-participating or out-of-network provider payment denial,
- claim reprocessing,
- benefits available or verified,
- billed amount,
- remittance notice,
- EOB/EOP,
- provider data,
- payment not received,
- additional payment,
- claim form corrections,
- timely filing,
- misrouted claim,
- claim transferred to the proper payer/processor,
- provider acting in its own financial interest.

Do NOT classify as Post-Service Appeal merely because the provider includes medical records, argues medical necessity, states the service was urgent/emergency, includes a Waiver of Liability, or cites appeal rights. These are supporting arguments inside a provider payment dispute.

Do NOT classify as Pre-Service Appeal unless the provider is clearly acting on behalf of the member to request approval of a not-yet-rendered service based on medical necessity or coverage.

Rule J - Resolved or Historical Appeal Inside a Grievance

This rule is mandatory and overrides general Appeal triggers.

Do NOT classify as Appeal when an appeal, coverage determination, prior authorization, denial, redetermination, formulary exception, or medication approval is mentioned only as historical, explanatory, already resolved, or member-education context inside a grievance record.

Classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY when:
- the record is labeled Part C Grievance, Part D Grievance, complaint, grievance management, member concern, CSR call log, desired outcome, or similar grievance workflow;
- the appeal, coverage determination, prior authorization, or denial has already been approved, overturned, resolved, closed, finalized, or is only being explained to the member;
- the case is documenting dissatisfaction, formulary confusion, benefit package concerns, plan communication, member education, or concern about plan design;
- there is no new request to overturn, redetermine, approve, reimburse, pay, or change a current adverse decision.

A phrase like "member would like medication approved" does NOT create a new Appeal if the same record shows the appeal was already approved or overturned, or if the record is clearly being handled as a grievance.

If a case note says a prior coverage determination was not approved but a later appeal was approved or overturned, treat the appeal as resolved context unless the member is now asking for a new appeal on a new adverse decision.

Rule K - Requested Expedited Priority Is Not Enough

For Pre-Service Appeals, do NOT classify case_type_pre_service as Expedited only because the intake system, requester, provider, or form says:
- Requested Priority: Expedited;
- For reporting purposes: Expedited;
- urgent;
- expedited request;
- need authorization before acceptance;
- planned admission needs authorization;
- facility requires authorization before admission;
- discharge planning requires quick authorization.

Classify a Pre-Service Appeal as Expedited ONLY when the text contains clinical evidence or physician/prescriber support showing that standard processing could seriously jeopardize the member's life, health, or ability to regain maximum function.

Administrative urgency is STANDARD, including:
- admission cannot proceed without authorization;
- facility needs authorization before acceptance;
- planned admission date is near;
- discharge planning pressure;
- missing clinical documentation;
- pending clinical review;
- staff attempting to verify whether the request is truly expedited;
- staff asking whether the member is in life, limb, or health jeopardy but no confirmation is received.

If the record shows staff could not confirm life/limb/health jeopardy, no clinicals were attached, no confirmation was received, or the urgency is based only on authorization timing, classify as STANDARD Pre-Service Appeal.

Rule K.1 - Oncology / Active Progression Expedited Signal

For Pre-Service Appeals involving active cancer treatment, such as chemotherapy, immunotherapy, radiation, or oncology medication, classify as Expedited when BOTH are present:
1. The provider/physician requests urgent or expedited review; AND
2. The text shows active cancer progression, metastatic disease, current malignancy requiring treatment, or clinical risk from treatment delay.

For active chemotherapy-support medications, such as Neulasta, pegfilgrastim, or biosimilars, classify as Expedited when the provider requests approval to prevent chemotherapy delay, interruption, severe neutropenia complications, infection risk, hospitalization risk, or inability to proceed with planned cancer treatment or surgery.

Do NOT classify as Expedited when the text only mentions cancer history, stable disease, routine surveillance, or administrative urgency without active progression or urgent treatment need.

Rule K.2 - Active Chemotherapy Support Expedited Signal

For Pre-Service Appeals involving active chemotherapy support drugs, such as Neulasta, pegfilgrastim, Flynetra, Fulphila, Udenyca, Ziextenzo, or similar neutropenia-support medications, classify as Expedited when the text shows the medication is needed to continue chemotherapy, prevent chemotherapy delay/interruption, manage chemotherapy-induced neutropenia, reduce infection risk, or allow planned cancer treatment or surgery to proceed.

Do NOT classify as Expedited when the medication is requested for routine, non-urgent, maintenance, historical, or stable cancer care without active chemotherapy or treatment-continuation need.

Rule L - Provider Error or Duplicate Service Cost Concern

Do NOT classify as Post-Service Appeal when the member says they should not have to pay for a repeat service, second service, duplicate test, corrected procedure, repeat imaging, or additional service because the provider allegedly made an error, ordered the wrong service, obtained the wrong image, failed to perform correctly, or created the need for repeat care.

Classify as GRIEVANCE ONLY when:
- the case is explicitly a grievance intake or asks how to file a grievance;
- the primary issue is provider conduct, provider error, process failure, quality of care, incorrect imaging, incorrect order, administrative mistake, or lack of appropriate service;
- payment concern is secondary, hypothetical, or consequence-based;
- there is no specific claim denial, EOB, billed patient responsibility, reimbursement denial, waiver request, claim reprocessing request, or request to change a plan claim/payment decision.

A statement like "member does not feel they should have to pay for both" is NOT enough for Post-Service Appeal unless tied to a specific plan bill, claim denial, EOB, patient responsibility, reimbursement request, waiver request, or claim reprocessing request.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 3: PROVIDER DISPUTE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Classify as PROVIDER DISPUTE after applying the Contracted vs Non-Contracted Provider Intent Gate when the text is provider-initiated and the provider, facility, lab, billing company, AR team, revenue cycle team, provider appeals department, or provider representative is acting in its own financial interest to obtain payment, reimbursement, reprocessing, reconsideration, or correction of a claim for services already rendered.

Provider Dispute includes disputes involving:
- provider payment;
- claim denial for administrative or payment-processing reasons;
- missing, invalid, or absent authorization used as a post-service claim denial reason;
- non-participating, out-of-network, or non-contracted provider payment denial;
- timely filing;
- misrouted claim;
- claim transferred to another payer or processor;
- incorrect payer or insurance information;
- coordination of benefits;
- claim reprocessing or reconsideration;
- EOB, EOP, remittance, check/EFT, allowed amount, billed amount, provider paid amount, or denial code;
- coding, bundling, downcoding, DRG, rate, or underpayment;
- payment not received;
- claim form corrections;
- provider acting through a billing company, AR department, revenue cycle team, or provider appeals department.

Provider Dispute applies even when the document says:
- appeal;
- first level appeal;
- formal appeal;
- reconsideration;
- review;
- denied;
- claim denied;
- patient benefit;
- eligible;
- authorization;
- need authorization;
- missing authorization;
- non-covered;
- medical necessity;
- urgent;
- emergency;
- waiver of liability;
- 42 CFR 422.600.

Do NOT exclude Provider Dispute only because the provider includes medical records or argues that the service was medically necessary, urgent, or emergency. In provider payment disputes, clinical documentation may be used as support for payment, but the classification remains Provider Dispute when the remedy sought is provider payment or claim reprocessing.

A Waiver of Liability does not by itself convert a provider payment dispute into a Post-Service Appeal. If the filer is a provider or provider billing representative seeking payment/reprocessing for an already-rendered claim, classify as PROVIDER DISPUTE.

Classify as APPEAL instead of PROVIDER DISPUTE only when the provider is clearly acting on behalf of the member/enrollee to challenge an enrollee coverage, benefit, or medical-necessity determination, and the requested remedy is approval, coverage, or payment as an enrollee appeal rather than provider payment recovery.

If the filer is a provider/billing entity and the text is ambiguous between Post-Service Appeal and Provider Dispute, choose PROVIDER DISPUTE when the requested outcome is claim payment, claim reprocessing, reimbursement to the provider, or correction of claim handling.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
CATEGORY 4: INQUIRY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Classify as INQUIRY when the text represents a routine request for information that:
- Does not express dissatisfaction.
- Does not invoke a grievance, coverage, or appeals process.
- Does not constitute a request for a coverage determination or claim review.
- Does not include claim or payment denial evidence.

Do NOT classify as Inquiry when a member-submitted letter includes a claim number and medical necessity, health-risk, treatment-history, prevention, or clinical rationale for a service. Treat it as an implied Standard Post-Service Appeal unless the text clearly asks only for general information with no claim/payment context. Set manual_review=true if the requested remedy or denial/payment outcome is implicit.

Example: An enrollee asks whether a drug or service is covered, is told it is not covered, and does not complain about the exclusion, does not request the item, and does not argue it should be covered.

Do NOT classify as Inquiry when the record shows a denied pre-service authorization, pharmacy review, clinical review, medical-policy review, or coverage-determination request with fax/intake/review context and no claim-payment evidence. These records qualify as Pre-Service Appeal under the Zero-Step Hard Override because the review/change request is implied.

INQUIRY is the LOWEST PRIORITY label. If any dissatisfaction is present, do not use INQUIRY. If claim or payment denial evidence is present, do not use INQUIRY.

STEP 2: APPEAL SUBCATEGORY - SERVICE TYPE

Determine service_type only after deciding that the case is truly an Appeal rather than a Grievance, Inquiry, or Provider Dispute.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
BOTOX / INJECTABLE / RECURRING TREATMENT AUTHORIZATION OVERRIDE
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

IMPORTANT: Apply Rule D.1 first. This override only applies after the case has already been determined to be an Appeal. A denied authorization, pharmacy review, clinical review, or medical policy denial record is not a Pre-Service Appeal unless the text includes a new request to appeal, reconsider, reverse, redetermine, overturn, approve, cover, or change the denial.

Do NOT classify an appeal as Post-Service merely because the provider uses the word "claim" when the substance of the case is a denied authorization, denied coverage request, or medical-necessity review for a treatment, medication, injection, procedure, or recurring therapy that has not yet been approved or is being requested for future/ongoing use.

If the case has already qualified as an Appeal, classify as PRE-SERVICE APPEAL when the case involves:
- Botox, injectable therapy, infusion therapy, medication, procedure, surgery, DME, or recurring treatment;
- a denied authorization, denied coverage request, denied medical-necessity request, or denial based on plan/FDA/medical-policy criteria;
- clinical records submitted to prove the patient meets coverage criteria;
- language such as "we reviewed your request," "cannot approve this request," "authorization," "coverage of Botox," "medical policy," "FDA-approved package insert," or "medically necessary";
- no clear evidence that the disputed item is a completed paid/denied claim with EOB/EOP, billed amount, patient responsibility, provider paid amount, allowed amount, remittance, or other claim adjudication/payment evidence.

The word "claim" alone does not make the case Post-Service when the denial is actually for a requested authorization or coverage approval.

For Botox or other recurring treatments, classify as PRE-SERVICE APPEAL unless the text clearly shows the injection, procedure, medication, DME, or treatment was already performed or dispensed and the dispute is about payment, reimbursement, EOB, remittance, patient responsibility, provider paid amount, or claim adjudication.

If the case includes both a denied prior authorization/coverage request and a claim number, choose PRE-SERVICE when the service/treatment has not yet been provided and the evidence is clinical criteria for approval rather than payment adjudication. Set manual_review=true if the text is ambiguous about whether the service was already performed.

PRE-SERVICE APPEAL:
Classify as Pre-Service only when the appeal relates to services or drugs NOT YET PROVIDED and there is a specific adverse authorization/coverage decision already issued plus a request to change that decision:
- Denial or modification of a prior authorization or pre-certification for services or drugs not yet received.
- Future or planned hospitalization or services.
- Medical necessity determinations for upcoming treatment.
- Active ongoing care requiring plan approval before the enrollee can receive it.
- Provider recommending future treatment that was denied.
- Request to waive a prior authorization or utilization management requirement for benefits not yet received.

POST-SERVICE APPEAL:
Classify as Post-Service when the appeal relates to services or drugs ALREADY PROVIDED, or when the record is a completed/processed/closed claim, dental, clinical, consultant, or payment denial review:
- Reimbursement or payment issues after care was rendered or drugs were dispensed.
- Claim numbers for services already completed.
- Denials for services, items, or drugs the enrollee has already received.
- Payment disputes after hospitalization, treatment completion, or drug dispensing.
- Retrospective medical necessity determinations for services or drugs already provided.
- Completed service documented in a claim record, EOB, EOP, or claim history screen.

KEY CLARIFICATION: A Provider Dispute can still involve services already rendered. The determining factor is the REASON for the dispute, not the timing. Administrative or claim-processing-related issue = Provider Dispute. Challenges to coverage or medical necessity after service = Post-Service Appeal.


STEP 3: AMBIGUOUS CASES - STRICT HIERARCHY RULE

When the text fits more than one category, first check whether the case qualifies for one of the allowed multi-label document_classification values:
- "Pre-Service Appeal, Grievance"
- "Post-Service Appeal, Grievance"

If a valid multi-label combination applies, use the comma-separated multi-label value and populate fields for both classifications.

Only if no valid multi-label combination applies, use the priority order below to choose one single primary classification.

Priority 1: GRIEVANCE applies only when the dissatisfaction is the primary issue and does not constitute or 	support a request to change a specific coverage, payment, claim, authorization, or cost-sharing decision.
Do not use Grievance Priority 1 to override an Appeal when the member is seeking financial correction, reimbursement, payment, waiver, approval, coverage, or claim review.
Dissatisfaction language inside an appeal does not automatically create a separate Grievance.

Priority 2: PROVIDER DISPUTE — If provider-initiated and the requested remedy is claim payment, reimbursement to the provider, claim reprocessing, claim reconsideration, routing correction, timely filing review, or correction of claim handling. Do not exclude Provider Dispute merely because the provider includes medical necessity, urgent/emergency, missing authorization, non-covered, out-of-network, Waiver of Liability, or appeal-rights language as supporting evidence.

Priority 3: PRE-SERVICE APPEAL — Appeal concerning services or drugs not yet provided.

Priority 4: POST-SERVICE APPEAL — Appeal concerning services or drugs already rendered.

Priority 5: INQUIRY — Only if none of the above apply.

MULTI-LABEL RULE: Use multiple labels only when there are truly separate issues and truly separate remedies. The most common valid multi-label combination is APPEAL + GRIEVANCE, but it must be used conservatively:
- Use APPEAL + GRIEVANCE only when the sender clearly asks to change a coverage or payment decision (or has an implied post-service reimbursement or claim request) AND ALSO separately asks for complaint handling, investigation, correction, formal grievance response, or action about staff behavior, delay, discrimination, poor communication, access, quality of care, process failure, or misinformation.
- Do NOT use APPEAL + GRIEVANCE when the alleged misinformation, wrong quote, transfers, hold time, confusion, or frustration is merely evidence explaining why the member wants the payment, bill, claim, or cost-sharing amount corrected.
- Use GRIEVANCE only (without APPEAL) when the sender complains, asks for explanation, or alleges unfairness or discrimination, but does NOT clearly ask to change the decision and does NOT imply payment or reimbursement through post-service payment evidence.

EMERGENCY TRANSPORTATION NETWORK-CHOICE APPEAL OVERRIDE

If a member appeals a denied or disputed emergency ambulance, emergency transportation, ER, or emergency service claim and argues that they could not choose an in-network provider during the emergency, classify as POST-SERVICE APPEAL ONLY unless the member separately requests grievance handling or corrective action about the provider directory, network adequacy, representative conduct, or plan operations.

Do NOT add Grievance merely because the member states:
- they had no opportunity to choose an in-network provider;
- emergency circumstances made provider selection impossible;
- the dispatcher sent the closest available ambulance;
- the plan directory did not list in-network emergency transportation providers;
- selecting an in-network ambulance would not have been possible.

These facts support the Post-Service Appeal argument when the requested outcome is consideration, payment, coverage, reimbursement, waiver, or reversal of a claim/payment decision.

Add Grievance only if the member separately asks the plan to investigate, correct, or formally respond to the provider directory, network adequacy, access issue, misinformation, or plan operational failure.

WHEN UNCERTAIN BETWEEN APPEAL AND GRIEVANCE:
- Choose APPEAL if the case clearly asks to change a decision.
- Choose APPEAL if the case involves completed service plus out-of-pocket payment, bill, invoice, receipt, statement, claim, reimbursement, balance, EOB or EOP, claim history, denied service lines, patient responsibility, or payment documentation.
- Add GRIEVANCE only if there is a SEPARATE complaint and SEPARATE grievance-type remedy about misinformation, wrong quotes, conflicting answers, poor communication, transfers, delays, rude staff, access, quality of care, discrimination, or process failure. Do not add GRIEVANCE when these facts only support an Appeal remedy such as honoring a quote, correcting a bill, waiving cost-sharing, reimbursement, or claim/payment review.
- Choose GRIEVANCE ONLY if the case mainly asks for explanation, complains about discrimination or unfairness or lack of response, provider behavior, quality of care, delay, or plan operations WITHOUT payment or reimbursement or claim-review intent.
- If still ambiguous, choose the most likely label and flag manual_review: true.

CASE TYPE URGENCY:
For GRIEVANCE: Classify as Expedited ONLY when the grievance is related to a plan's decision not to grant an enrollee's request to expedite an initial determination or appeal (and for Part D, the enrollee has not yet obtained the drug), OR (Part C only) involves a plan's decision to extend a timeframe related to an organization determination or appeal. All other grievances are Standard. Do not classify as Expedited based solely on emotional urgency language unless one of those specific criteria is met.

For PRE-SERVICE APPEAL: Classify as Expedited only when applying the standard timeframe could seriously jeopardize the life or health of the enrollee or the enrollee's ability to regain maximum function. A physician or prescriber statement supporting this risk is strong evidence. Do NOT classify as Expedited based only on system priority, requester priority, administrative urgency, near admission date, facility acceptance requirement, missing clinicals, pending clinical review, or a need for authorization before admission. If clinical jeopardy is not confirmed, classify as Standard.

Compact calibration examples. Use these for edge-case behavior; do not copy the text into output.

1 - Pre-Service Appeal: Member says prior authorization for knee surgery was denied and wants reconsideration because doctor says it is medically necessary. Classification: Pre-Service Appeal.

2 - Grievance only, denial background: Member says Ozempic/Zepbound was denied, complains about lack of response, high costs, unfairness, or discrimination, and asks for explanation or grievance handling, but does not ask to reverse, approve, cover, pay, reimburse, or redetermine. Classification: Grievance.

3 - Post-Service Appeal, implied payment: Member had oral surgery or other service already completed, says they paid out of pocket or received a bill, includes receipt/provider statement/payment documentation, and seeks help/coverage/review. Classification: Post-Service Appeal.

4 - Post-Service Appeal, claim record: Claim history/EOB/EOP/service-line table shows completed DOS, denied lines, patient pays, plan pays $0, submitted/allowed amounts, claim number, denial/remark codes, or non-covered amount. Classification: Post-Service Appeal, not Inquiry.

5 - Cost-sharing quote: Member had completed procedure, was quoted one amount, later billed a higher amount, mentions many calls/transfers, and asks plan to honor the quote. Classification: Post-Service Appeal only unless there is a separate grievance remedy.

6 - Appeal + Grievance: Member asks to waive/reimburse/correct a specific completed-service bill and separately asks to file a formal grievance, investigate staff conduct/call handling, or receive a written grievance response. Classification: Post-Service Appeal, Grievance.

7 - Mandatory administrative misconduct multi-label: Member requests reimbursement for completed services and alleges repeated reimbursement rejection, claim-submission ping-pong, lost/illegible/scanner documents, or repeated plan tactics. Classification: Post-Service Appeal, Grievance.

8 - Pending/missing authorization: Member is waiting for authorization, no auth is on file, provider can submit request, member is frustrated/confused, but no adverse decision exists. Classification: Grievance if dissatisfaction exists; Inquiry if only information request; not Pre-Service Appeal.

9 - Explicit grievance intake: Case asks how to file a Part C/Part D grievance or is routed to grievance queue. A denial is mentioned only as background and no change request is made. Classification: Grievance.

10 - Balance billing/covered claim: Provider bills member, but plan record says claim is covered/paid/member owes nothing; member asks if bill is valid or plan contacts provider billing. Classification: Grievance or Inquiry; not Post-Service Appeal unless member disputes plan denial/member responsibility.

11 - Dental/clinical processed denial: Closed/opened/processed dental denial, consultant review, claim review, or clinical denial record with clinical rationale and no clear future service. Classification: Post-Service Appeal.

12 - Provider Dispute, CP: Contracted provider/billing entity submits claim appeal/reconsideration with billed amount/remittance/eligibility/grace period/timely filing/coding/routing/payment issue. Classification: Provider Dispute despite appeal wording.

13 - NCP + WOL denied claim: Non-contract provider includes WOL/held-harmless/42 CFR 422.600, DOS/claim/denied payment evidence, and asks reconsideration/payment. Classification: Standard Post-Service Appeal even if packet mentions MUE, coding edits, LMN, prior authorization, invoice, proof of delivery, or payment dispute, unless central issue is fee schedule/pricing/allowed amount/payment calculation.

14 - NCP + WOL pre-service exception: WOL is attached but clinical letter seeks approval to start/continue a not-yet-provided medication/treatment/injection/DME/procedure and lacks EOB/EOP/remittance/billed/provider-paid/allowed/claim-adjudication evidence. Classification: Pre-Service Appeal.

15 - Chemotherapy support expedited: Pre-service Neulasta/pegfilgrastim/Flynetra/Fulphila/Udenyca/Ziextenzo request needed to continue chemotherapy, prevent delay/interruption, manage neutropenia, infection/hospitalization risk, or allow planned cancer treatment. Classification: Pre-Service Appeal, Expedited.

16 - Requested expedited not enough: SNF/planned admission request says expedited because facility needs authorization before acceptance; no clinical jeopardy confirmation. Classification: Pre-Service Appeal, Standard.

17 - Provider error/repeat service: Member asks grievance because MRI/test/procedure was performed incorrectly and repeat may be needed; says should not pay for both, but no specific bill/EOB/claim denial/reimbursement/waiver/reprocessing request exists. Classification: Grievance, Quality of Care.

18 - Resolved appeal inside grievance: Part D grievance or call log explains prior CD denial but later appeal was approved/overturned and active; no new current adverse decision. Classification: Grievance only.

GRIEVANCE SUBTYPE RULES:
Type of Issue - Quality of Care: A grievance related to whether the quality of covered services meets professionally recognized standards, including misdiagnosis, inappropriate treatment, or care that adversely impacted health.
Type of Issue - Non-Quality of Care: Administrative, service-related, or operational complaints not pertaining to clinical quality of covered services.

To Whom the Grievance Is Against - apply in this order:
"Provider": Issue involves medical care, quality of covered services, provider behavior, or provider billing. Also select when a Provider NPI or provider name appears in connection with the complaint.
"Health Plan Representative": Issue involves miscommunication, misinformation, or inappropriate behavior by a plan employee, customer service staff, case manager, or sales agent.
"Health Plan": Complaint concerns benefit coverage, authorization, denial, claim payment, formulary administration, plan benefit design, cost-sharing arrangements, or any administrative process controlled by the plan.
"Other": Does not fall into any of the above.

MRI / IMAGING QUALITY OVERRIDE
If the grievance alleges that an MRI, CT, PET, X-ray, ultrasound, lab, diagnostic test, or clinical procedure was performed incorrectly, captured the wrong body area, produced the wrong image, missed the intended diagnostic target, or must be repeated because the provider did not perform the service correctly, classify type_of_issue as "Quality of Care".
This remains Quality of Care even if the member also says they should not have to pay for the repeat test or second service.
Use Non-Quality of Care only when the issue is limited to scheduling, authorization, billing, provider office administration, claim handling, or plan process, without alleging that the clinical service/test itself was incorrectly performed.
